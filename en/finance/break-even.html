How to Calculate Your Business Break-Even Point

The break-even point tells you the exact sales volume your business needs to cover all expenses without making a profit or taking a loss. Knowing this number is essential before launching a product, adjusting prices, or taking on new fixed commitments such as rent or payroll:

  • Fixed costs: Recurring monthly expenses that remain constant regardless of sales volume (rent, administrative salaries, utilities, internet, software subscriptions).
  • Variable costs: Direct costs tied to each unit produced or sold (raw materials, wholesale inventory, packaging, payment processing fees, shipping).

Practical Example: $2,500 Fixed Costs, $50 Unit Price, and $25 Variable Cost

Suppose your business carries $2,500 in monthly fixed overhead, sells a product for $50 per unit, and incurs $25 in variable costs per unit:

  • Unit contribution margin: $50 - $25 = $25.00 per unit (a 50.0% contribution margin ratio).
  • Break-even volume in units: $2,500 / $25 = 100 units per month.
  • Break-even sales revenue: 100 units × $50 = $5,000.00 in sales. Starting at unit 101, every sale adds $25 in pre-tax operating profit.

Formulas Used

Break-Even Units = (Fixed Costs + Target Profit) / (Unit Price - Unit Variable Cost)
Break-Even Revenue ($) = Break-Even Units × Unit Price

Leaving Target Profit at $0 gives you the exact operational threshold where total revenue equals total costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a business break-even point?

It is the exact sales volume (in units or revenue) where total revenue equals total costs (fixed plus variable). Every unit sold beyond that threshold generates net operating profit.

What is unit contribution margin?

It is the difference between a product's unit selling price and its unit variable cost. It shows how much money each sale contributes toward covering fixed overhead.

How do I calculate sales volume needed for a target profit?

Add your desired monthly profit to total fixed costs before dividing by the unit contribution margin: Required Units = (Fixed Costs + Target Profit) / (Unit Price - Unit Variable Cost).

What can I do if my break-even point is too high?

You have three main levers: reduce monthly fixed overhead, lower unit variable costs through better supplier pricing, or raise your unit selling price by increasing perceived product value.
