Amortized vs. Simple Interest Loans

When comparing financing offers, knowing how interest is calculated can save you significant money:

  • Amortized (French / Reducing Balance): Used by banks for mortgages, auto loans, and standard personal loans. Interest is calculated each month solely on your remaining principal balance.
  • Simple (Flat-Rate Interest): Common in short-term commercial or direct financing. Total interest is calculated upfront on the entire initial principal across all months and divided equally.

Formulas Used

Amortized: M = P × [ r(1 + r)^n ] / [ (1 + r)^n - 1 ]
Simple: M = [ P + (P × r × n) ] / n

Where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the total number of monthly payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between amortized and simple interest loans?

In an amortized loan (reducing balance), monthly interest is charged only on your remaining principal balance, lowering total interest paid. In a simple (flat-rate) loan, interest is calculated on the full original principal for the entire term, resulting in higher total interest.

How can I lower the total interest paid on a loan?

Choosing a shorter loan term, opting for a reducing-balance amortized loan, or making extra principal-only payments significantly cuts total interest.
