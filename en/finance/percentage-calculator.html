Quick Guide to Calculating Percentages, Discounts, and Sales Tax

Whether you are shopping retail sales, invoicing with sales tax or VAT, or measuring price and revenue shifts, mastering the three core percentage operations prevents costly math mistakes. You can also save every calculation to your local history list and export the entire table to Excel or PDF:

  • Applying a retail discount: Multiply the original price by (1 - Discount % / 100). For instance, a 25% discount means multiplying the list price by 0.75. When sales tax applies, it is calculated on the discounted subtotal.
  • Finding a direct percentage (X% of Y): Multiply the base amount by the target percentage and divide by 100. Useful for sales commissions, tips, withholdings, or standalone taxes.
  • Percentage change (From X to Y): Measure how much a metric grew or dropped relative to its starting value using [(Final - Initial) / Initial] × 100.

Practical Example: $120 Item With 25% Off + 16% Sales Tax

Suppose a product is listed at $120.00 with a 25% discount and an additional 16% sales tax / VAT:

  • 1. Discount savings: $120.00 × 25% = -$30.00.
  • 2. Discounted subtotal: $120.00 - $30.00 = $90.00.
  • 3. Sales tax / VAT (16%): $90.00 × 16% = +$14.40.
  • 4. Final price to pay: $90.00 + $14.40 = $104.40.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the final price with both a discount and sales tax?

First subtract the discount percentage from the original price to get the discounted subtotal, and then apply the sales tax or VAT percentage to that reduced subtotal.

How is percentage change calculated between two values?

Subtract the starting value from the final value, divide that difference by the starting value, and multiply by 100. A positive number indicates an increase, while a negative number indicates a decrease.

How do you extract the pre-tax price from a tax-inclusive total?

Divide the total price by (1 + tax rate in decimal form). For example, if an item costs $116 including 16% tax, divide 116 by 1.16 to get the pre-tax price of $100.

Is a 20% discount plus an extra 10% off the same as a 30% discount?

No. Stacked discounts apply sequentially to the remaining balance: 20% off leaves 80%, and taking 10% off that 80% removes another 8%, resulting in a combined discount of 28%.
