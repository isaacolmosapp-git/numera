Profit Margin vs. Markup: How to Price Products Accurately

One of the most expensive pricing mistakes in retail and small business is confusing gross profit margin with markup on cost, which leads to underpricing inventory and shrinking net profits:

  • Profit Margin: The percentage of the net selling price that remains as gross profit after covering the unit cost of the item.
  • Markup: The percentage added on top of your unit cost to set the selling price. Markup is always higher than the resulting profit margin.

Practical Example: $50 Unit Cost With 30% Target Margin and 16% Sales Tax

Suppose you source or manufacture an item for $50.00 and want a true 30% net profit margin where a 16% sales tax / VAT applies:

  • 1. Net selling price (before tax): $50.00 / (1 - 0.30) = $71.43 (simply adding 30% to cost would price it at $65.00, yielding just a 23.1% margin).
  • 2. Net profit per unit: $71.43 - $50.00 = $21.43 (equivalent to a 42.9% markup over cost).
  • 3. Final customer price (with tax): $71.43 × 1.16 = $82.86.

Formulas Used

Net Price = Unit Cost / (1 - Margin %)
Final Price = Net Price × (1 + Tax %)

Where Margin % and Tax % are expressed as decimals (for example, 30% = 0.30 and 16% = 0.16).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is sales tax or VAT applied at the end?

Sales tax or VAT is collected on behalf of tax authorities and is not part of your revenue or commercial margin. Therefore, the net selling price is calculated first based on your target margin, and tax is added afterward.

What is a good profit margin?

It varies by industry: retail businesses typically aim for 25% to 50% gross margins, whereas professional services, consulting, or digital software often exceed 70% due to low variable costs.

Why doesn't adding 30% to my cost give me a 30% profit margin?

Multiplying your cost by 1.30 applies a 30% markup on cost rather than margin on selling price, leaving you with only a 23.08% profit margin. To achieve a true 30% profit margin, you must divide your unit cost by 0.70 (1 - 0.30).
